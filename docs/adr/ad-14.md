# AD-14 — Bound mutations by durable checkpoints and atomic filesystem acts

- Status: accepted.
- API-007 clarification: [decision 114](decision-114.md) adds atomic local
  admission receipts, separate from the archive mutations/journals governed here.
- ADR backfill: 2026-10-17; original decision and implementation dates are
  recorded below.
- Authority: the cited architecture contract remains normative. This ADR
  records rationale and history; maintain it under [the ADR policy](README.md).

## Context

A writer can crash after any filesystem operation. Correctness cannot
depend on an entire sync, prune, repair, or recovery command finishing,
nor on a journal recording an intention that the filesystem never reached.

## Decision

Open a durable transaction journal before transaction-owned mutation.
Record append-only checkpoints of durable filesystem facts after each
specified operation. Use same-filesystem atomic renames at publication and
tombstoning boundaries, with the documented fsync and cleanup primitives.
Recovery interprets each operation's checkpoint sequence and commit point.

## Rationale and alternatives

An enumerated on-disk state and its durable evidence allow one safe
recovery action after interruption. Atomic replacement keeps consumer
references on a complete old or new artifact.

The related FW-01/FW-02 decision rejects a workflow engine or a journal of
normal-operation orchestration intentions. The filesystem is the authority;
recovery observes what actually happened rather than replaying what a
process hoped to do.

## Consequences

Checkpoint vocabulary and order are durable contracts tested against the
architecture tables. Ambiguity stops mutation before Hub contact. Prune
and purge share the logical commit boundary: earlier recovery restores
content; later recovery completes audit and cleanup without republishing.
The narrowly scoped explicit cleanup of already resolved redundant staging
uses repeatable evidence validation rather than a new transaction journal.
