# AD-04 — Bound retention to three revisions by default and preserve explicit five

- Status: accepted (amended 2026-09-14).
- ADR backfill: 2026-10-17; original decision and implementation dates are
  recorded below.
- Authority: the cited architecture contract remains normative. This ADR
  records rationale and history; maintain it under [the ADR policy](../../../../../adr/README.md).

## Context

The archive needs predictable storage growth and enough recent history for
rollback. The original schema-1 policy retained five unique revisions; an
operator later requested three as the default while keeping existing
explicit-five configurations usable.

## Decision

Retain at most `storage.maximum_revisions` unique published SHAs per
repository, defaulting to three and accepting exactly three or five. A
successful new publication beyond the configured limit makes the oldest
eligible revision a prune candidate. Never prune the current revision.

## Rationale and alternatives

A small fixed policy bounds storage while preserving recent rollback
choices. Supporting explicit five avoids invalidating deployed
configurations when the default changes.

Decision 102 rejects arbitrary retention counts because they exceed the
requested policy change; rejecting existing explicit-five configurations
because it breaks upgrades unnecessarily; and manual archive deletion
because it bypasses ownership, audit, and recovery.

## Consequences

Publication generation, rather than time, determines oldest eligibility
(AD-05). Prune remains transactional and may over-retain safely after a
failure. The schema-1 audit reason `sixth_unique_revision_published` remains
its historical name even when the configured limit is three. Rolling back
to a pre-RET-010 binary requires an explicit value of five; already pruned
revisions do not reappear.
